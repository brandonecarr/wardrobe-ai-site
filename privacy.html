Wardrobe AI privacy policy

Effective 5 November 2026. Wardrobe AI is made by Brandon Carr. This page says what the app stores, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

The short version

Wardrobe AI keeps a private catalogue of your clothes and suggests one outfit a day. Everything it stores is tied to your account and visible only to you. There is no advertising, no tracking across apps or sites, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

What the app stores

  • Sign-in. Your email address, or the identifier Apple gives us when you use Sign in with Apple. Used only to sign you in and keep your data separate from everyone else's.
  • Your wardrobe. Garment names, categories, colours, fabrics, seasons and the photos you choose to add (front and, if you like, back). Photos are stored privately; no one else can open them.
  • Photos of you (optional). Up to four reference photos (front, back, left, right) if you set up try-on previews. They are stored privately, used only to render previews you ask for, and you can remove any of them at any time from Profile → My photos.
  • Outfits and feedback. The outfit suggested each day, whether you wore, skipped or swapped it, and the rating you give it. This is what the app learns from.
  • Style profile. Your quiz answers and the preferences the app derives from your feedback.
  • Weather location. If you allow location, your approximate position is used on the device to fetch today's forecast and is not stored on our servers. If you type in a city instead, that city is saved to your profile so your other devices can use it.
  • Reminder times, if you turn reminders on. Notifications are scheduled on your phone.

Who else sees any of it

  • Supabase hosts the database, photo storage and sign-in (servers in the United States).
  • Anthropic receives a garment photo when you ask the app to recognise it, and a short list of your garments and the day's context when it composes an outfit. These requests are used to answer you, not to train models.
  • OpenAI receives your reference photos and the outfit's garment images only when you tap "See it on me" to render a try-on preview. The request is used to produce that image, not to train models.
  • Weather. Apple Weather or Open-Meteo receive coordinates (your location or your chosen city) to return a forecast. Nothing identifying you is sent with them.
  • Email delivery. The provider that sends sign-in codes receives your email address for that purpose only.
  • Crash reports. If crash reporting is enabled in a release, anonymous crash details (device model, OS version, the error) go to Sentry. No photos or wardrobe data are included.

What the app never does

  • No advertising identifiers, no ad networks, no analytics that track you across other apps or websites.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your data for marketing.
  • No reading of your photo library beyond the pictures you pick.

Your controls

  • Export. Profile → Account → Export my data gives you every record as a JSON file.
  • Delete. Profile → Account → Delete account and all data removes your photos, wardrobe, history, learning data and sign-in permanently, usually within a minute.
  • Permissions. Camera, photos, location and notifications can each be changed any time in iOS Settings; the app keeps working with whatever you allow.

Children

Wardrobe AI is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will say so the next time you open it. Questions: brandonecarr@gmail.com.
